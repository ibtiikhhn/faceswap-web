The opportunity

  • Face Changer targets replacing a face in an existing picture. Custom Face Swap targets choosing both uploads, especially the target scene.
  • Face changer has stronger measured demand: 1,900 estimated US searches/month versus 10 for custom face swap. The custom page is a product-fit page, not a proven large traffic opportunity.
  • Both pages use the existing working photo editor, real published input/output examples and distinct practical guidance.

Why these pages

Face changer: explain replacement before filters

  • Evidence: Google suggestions include “change face in photo with another face,” online/free variants and prompt searches. The existing face-changer question dataset also contains many animal, anime and funny-face filter questions.
  • Implementation: Explain source versus target, show a café replacement, distinguish retouching and filters, and answer prompt/mobile/free questions. Ten FAQs and four troubleshooting cases support the editor.
  • Expected effect: Searchers can identify whether face replacement solves their task before using the trial. No ranking gain is measured yet.

Custom: make the target choice useful

  • Evidence: Autocomplete surfaced custom portraits, posters, no-login use and own-photo swaps. Exact custom head-term demand is small; most proposed own-photo long tails have unknown volume.
  • Implementation: Explain custom targets, present the coastal example, show portrait/travel/poster preparation and a one-input-at-a-time variation workflow. Twelve FAQs cover practical controls and limits.
  • Expected effect: Help users arriving with their own scene understand how to use it, without adding a duplicate generic homepage.

Suggestions and AI-question evidence

  • Eight live Google autocomplete seeds returned 76 rows; raw results and exact endpoints are saved in google-suggestions.json.
  • Reused 20 owner-supplied OpenSEO face-changer questions from November 1. Seven relate to choosing tools or free access; thirteen concern filters, Snapchat or off-topic transformations. Underlying citation URLs were not supplied with that older batch.
  • Also reused the 58 broader face-swap questions saved during Task 4. Relevant examples include “How do i edit a photo to replace a face?” and “Is there a free tool for swapping faces?” They are broader questions, not evidence of custom-specific demand.
  • The dataset is mostly based on search questions such as People Also Ask. It is not private ChatGPT usage or prompt volume. No tracking or new answer collection was started.

Google suggestions about gaming, animal filters, health changes, unlimited access, APKs and video were excluded from supported feature claims. Questions about prompts are answered by explaining the actual two-upload interface.

  • Both new canonical URLs are included in the sitemap and footer.
  • Outdated automatic-expiry statements in the linked homepage and photo guide were corrected to match the current storage policy.

Risks and caveats

Photo, changer and custom intents overlap in current search results. Distinct content is a deliberate page-ownership choice, not proof Google treats them as separate markets. Revisit consolidation if Search Console shows persistent overlap without useful separate performance.

Examples are existing published assets, not newly generated results or a quality benchmark. No video, native app, expression slider, manual face assignment, unlimited free use or print resolution is promised.

What to do next

  1. Deploy and request indexing for /face-changer and /custom-face-swap.
  2. Watch which queries land on each URL after indexing; compare with /face-swap rather than assuming all traffic is incremental.
  3. Continue with Task 6 only after confirming the actual morph/edit workflows; do not describe blending controls that are not implemented.
