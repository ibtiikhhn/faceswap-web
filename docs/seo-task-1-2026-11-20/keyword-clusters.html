Writing and on-page keyword standards

ElementRequirementAvoid
TitlePrimary keyword near the beginning, a specific task or benefit, optional short brand. Every page gets a unique title.A string of synonyms such as AI face swap, face changer, face editor, free online.
H1One clear main heading using the primary keyword or its closest natural form.Vague slogans that leave the product/task unexplained.
Meta descriptionUse the primary phrase naturally, describe the actual workflow and include one concrete reason to click. Write separately for each page.Generic superlatives, unlimited-free claims, or copying the title with extra keywords.
Opening copyAnswer what the visitor can do, what inputs are needed and what happens next. Use the core term naturally.In today’s digital world; unlock your creativity; revolutionary; seamless perfection.
H2/H3Assign related terms to sections that answer different questions. Include primary or secondary phrases where they accurately describe the section.Putting the exact primary keyword in every heading or repeating an identical page outline for every synonym.
FAQUse genuine task questions and long-tail wording. Give a direct answer before detail. The questions in these briefs are proposed editorial questions, not claimed Google PAA results.Inventing measured demand for a question, or making every answer repeat the keyword.
Images / alt textDescribe the real source, target or result and visible distinction. Use relevant terms naturally; decorative images use empty alt text.Keyword lists, unrelated adult modifiers, repeated alt text, or describing an output that was never generated.
Examples and claimsOriginal inputs/outputs, labeled scenes and reproducible steps. Tie quality claims to visible evidence.Calling all outputs perfect, instant, HD, private, unlimited or best without evidence.
Internal anchorsConcise task-specific text to the owning money page. A reader should understand the destination.The same exact-match anchor everywhere or links to every money page from every article.
