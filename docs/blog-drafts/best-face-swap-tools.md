## How to choose an AI face swap app for realistic photos

A search for “best AI face swap app” can return mobile apps, browser tools, video products and desktop software. Narrow the choice by the result you need before comparing feature lists.

### Check whether you can upload both pictures

If you want to recreate a specific photo with your face, you need control over the source portrait and target image. A template-only experience may be entertaining but may not accept the exact picture you want.

SwapThisFace accepts your own two images and also offers optional templates. Our [two-photo face swap guide](/blog/how-to-swap-a-face-between-two-photos) explains which image goes in each field and how to review the finished result.

### Judge likeness and blending separately

An output can have smooth edges yet fail to look like you. It can also preserve recognizable features while placing them awkwardly on the target's head. Look for both identity and fit.

Inspect eye shape, mouth and overall facial proportions against the source. Then compare the output with the target for head angle, shadows, hairline and neck transitions. Enlarge the result rather than relying only on the preview.

### Look outside the face

Check clothing, accessories, text and background details. Do not assume everything outside the edited face remained identical. A small change near an ear or collar may matter if the result is intended for a close crop.

## How to get a better photo face swap on your first attempt

Start with a clear portrait where the face is visible, then select a target with a reasonably compatible head angle. Avoid a heavily filtered selfie or a target where the face occupies only a tiny part of the image.

1. **Choose the target scene first.** Decide which composition you want to recreate.
2. **Match the source to that target.** Look for compatible head direction, expression and lighting.
3. **Upload the original files.** Avoid repeatedly saving and re-uploading screenshots of previous outputs.
4. **Inspect both previews.** Make sure the source supplies your identity and the target supplies the scene.
5. **Review the result at full size.** Check the face and the surrounding details before keeping it.
6. **Change one input if necessary.** A different source can help you understand whether the first portrait was the problem.

A new attempt is not guaranteed to improve the result. If the target has a very small, obscured or strongly turned face, choosing another target may be more useful than repeating the same pair.
